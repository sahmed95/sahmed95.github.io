University of Washington  ·  Contract Theory & Structural Estimation

My research focuses on microeconomic theory and structural estimation. I study dynamic moral hazard and incentive design to understand how institutions and agents adapt when preferences and constraints evolve. Separately, I estimate high-dimensional demand systems, using regularization methods to address the collinearity and scale of modern real-world data.

About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the University of Washington, expecting to finish in Fall 2026, advised by Fahad Khalil and Jacques Lawarrée. My dissertation looks separately at dynamic moral hazard theory and structural estimation methods applied to high-dimensional demand settings.

I am on the industry job market this year.

In 2025 I was an Economist Intern at Amazon, where I built structural demand models on large-scale survey data for Project Kuiper and a simulation engine for evaluating counterfactual pricing and product scenarios. I hold an M.A. in Economics and an M.S. in Applied Mathematics from UW, and a B.A. in Mathematics and Economics from Colby College (Magna Cum Laude, Phi Beta Kappa).

Dissertation Chapters

Dynamic Moral Hazard and Incentive Design

Chapter 2 · Work in Progress

Dynamic Moral Hazard Under a Fixed Budget

How agents allocate spending across time under a budget constraint, and how this can explain budget hoarding and end-of-year spending spikes.

    NBA Decade Comparison: PCA and Machine Learning Analysis

    Built classifiers on 13,000 player-season observations, using roughly 95 features scraped from Basketball Reference.
